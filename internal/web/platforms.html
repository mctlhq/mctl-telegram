For agent platforms

Connect your users to Telegram

For agent platforms and MCP gateways that connect many users to {{.MCPURL}}: how a client is admitted, what it may do, the limits it runs under, and how access ends. The limits and the CIMD status below are read from this deployment's running configuration.

Who this is for

A platform that lets its own users bring a Telegram account into an AI agent: one OAuth client on your side, one Telegram sign-in per user, each user's access confined to their own account. Individual users connecting ChatGPT, Claude or Cursor need none of this page; /docs covers them.

Admitting your client

A platform's host is approved by the operator once. After that, every one of its users connects without further setup on either side. There are two ways in:

Client ID Metadata Document (preferred)

  • Your client_id is an https URL on your own domain, and your redirect URIs come from the JSON document served there. You change callbacks by editing your document, not by asking us.
  • The document must name its own URL as client_id, carry no client_secret, list 1 to 20 https redirect_uris, and use token_endpoint_auth_method none or private_key_jwt (RS256 or ES256, with jwks_uri on the same host).
  • Fetches are bounded: 64 KiB body, 10 second timeout, and Cache-Control honoured between 30 seconds and 24 hours. A document that cannot be fetched gives a retryable server_error; a CIMD client is never silently downgraded to DCR.
  • For private_key_jwt, the assertion's aud is the issuer {{.AuthServer}} or {{.AuthServer}}/oauth/token, lifetime at most 5 minutes, jti single-use.
  • On this deployment: {{if .CIMDAccept}}CIMD is accepted for approved hosts.{{else}}CIMD is not enabled.{{end}} {{if .CIMDAdvertise}}Discovery advertises it (client_id_metadata_document_supported).{{else}}Discovery does not advertise it yet, so a client that checks client_id_metadata_document_supported before using a URL client_id will register with DCR instead.{{end}}

Dynamic Client Registration

  • POST /oauth/register (RFC 7591) with redirect URIs on an approved host. Redirects are https, or http on loopback only; custom URL schemes are refused.
  • Registration is limited to 10 attempts per minute per source IP. Registrations are held in memory for up to 24 hours and do not survive a restart, so register when a user connects rather than once at install.

To have a host approved, write to support@mctl.ai with the host, whether you use CIMD or DCR, and your expected number of users.

Limits are per Telegram user, not per platform, so one busy user does not slow down the rest. Telegram's own flood limits apply on top and surface as errors with a retry delay.

The tool surface is a contract

  • Every tool carries readOnlyHint and destructiveHint annotations. The full surface, schemas included, is committed as docs/tool-descriptors.json.
  • A release cannot add, remove or change a tool without an approved entry in the change feed at /docs/product-updates; CI enforces it.

Errors your client will see

docs/troubleshooting.md lists the error families clients actually hit, with the fix for each: expired access tokens, a revoked Telegram session (AUTH_KEY_UNREGISTERED), Telegram flood limits, redirect URI refusals and transient Telegram failures.

Revocation and offboarding

  • Your client revokes a user's grant with POST /oauth/revoke, which ends that refresh-token family. Access tokens already issued expire on their own lifetime ({{.AccessTTL}}).
  • A user can disconnect their Telegram session on /telegram/connect/manage or with disconnect_telegram_account, and delete their account with delete_telegram_account or DELETE /api/account.
  • If an approved CIMD host is removed, its users' refreshes stop at the next token request.
Known gap. Deleting an account does not yet revoke the OAuth clients connected to it; the fix is tracked in #781. Until it ships, revoke with /oauth/revoke when a user leaves your platform.

Bring your own session: Local Bridge

For users or companies that will not leave a Telegram session on a hosted server, Local Bridge keeps it on the user's machine. The user installs mctl-telegram-local, signs in to Telegram locally and activates the device in a browser; your platform keeps the same endpoint and the same OAuth client. The server then relays MCP calls to that machine and sees the payloads in transit, so it is not end-to-end encryption. Local Bridge is in beta: the daemon must be running, and editing, deleting, forwarding, searching and reactions are not yet available in this mode.
